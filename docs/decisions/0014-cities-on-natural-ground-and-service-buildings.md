# 0014. Cities on natural ground, foundations, service buildings and a mountain city
- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-22

## Context
Cities were built on a level pad: the terrain under each wall was flattened to one height ([ADR 0012](0012-city-generator-import-and-placement.md), CLAUDE.md section 6.1 step 6), so no house ever needed support and no city could stand on a hillside. The developer asked for (1) cities on slopes and uneven ground, with a pier, pedestal or pilings (wooden logs or stacked stones) to make up the difference, (2) a fixed set of service buildings in every city (city hall, inn, blacksmith, trader, baker, farmer, woodcutter, jail, warehouse, ...), and (3) one more city in the mountains. The developer chose "no flatten at all" for the ground, a YAML content type for the services and a tiered set (all in cities, a core subset in towns).

## Options considered
1. Soft flatten (pull the ground part of the way to a level plane) — less terrain risk, but still alters the land. Kept as a setting (`FlattenStrength`, default 0).
2. No flatten, buildings follow the ground (chosen) — dramatic, honest terrain; every city mesh must follow the ground.
3. Keep the pad and cut terraces — least change, least "uneven".

## Decision
- **Ground**: `SettlementTerrain.Flatten` does nothing at the default `FlattenStrength` 0. Placement allows relief up to 0.2 x pad radius (was 0.1) and still picks the flattest site; `SettlementValidator` (SETL004) now checks relief and height range instead of levelness. `settlement.placement` is v3.
- **A baked ground grid** (`settlements_ground.bin`, 8 m spacing, sampled from the final terrain after roads) travels with the manifest so a city can be built on a worker thread without the terrain engine. `SettlementGround` turns it into an `IGroundSampler` in the city's plan frame (height above the settlement origin).
- **Buildings**: `BuildingSite.Plan` sets the floor just above the highest ground under the footprint (and in front of the door), so terrain never rises into a room. A building mesh is built with its floor at 0 and a foundation below it; the game places it at `BaseY`. `FoundationMesher` adds: a **stone plinth** (courses of stacked stone), **wooden pilings** (a post grid with beams under the walls) or a **pier** (pilings plus a rail-edged deck, for wooden foundations taller than 3.5 m), and **steps** from the door down to the street. Style: stone for market, merchant and cathedral wards; stone or wood (by building ID) for craftsmen; wood for slums and farms; a service's `foundation` overrides; a drop under 0.9 m is always stone, under 0.2 m none.
- **Wall, landmark**: there is no ground or street mesh (removed on the developer's request the same day; houses stand on the bare terrain and paths come later); the wall is cut into 8 m pieces tilted to follow the ground and sunk 2 m; the centrepiece stands at the ground height of the square with a stone plinth, and market stalls stand on the ground under them.
- **Service buildings**: a new content type `service` (`content/services/`, [reference](../reference/content/service.md)) lists, per tier, how many of a building a settlement gets, which wards it prefers, where in the ward (near the square, a gate or the wall), its minimum footprint, a foundation, and the room type of its main room. `ServiceCatalog` turns the records into `ServiceSpec`s stored in `settlements.json` (so the game needs no content loader). `ServiceAssigner` runs inside `CityGenerator` after the size trim, on its own random stream (so adding services never moves a house): inside the wall it picks existing lots by ward rank, nearness and a spread penalty between buildings of the same service; outside the wall (farmer, woodcutter) it builds a new house on a farmland patch next to the wall. A service building has a coloured sign over its door and its main room type. `ServiceValidator` (SETL007 to SETL009) checks that every service is present, in the right place and big enough.
- **Mountain city**: `SettlementSettings.MountainCities` (default 1) places an extra city in terrain whose biome has the tag `mountainous` (the bake passes a region test; without one, ground at or above 330 m counts), at 330 to 1,100 m with relief up to 0.35 x radius. It is a normal city (tier `City`, id `settlement.mountain_N`, `Highland` true) and comes after the other cities in the list, so the spawn stays in the first city.
- **Roads**: roads are planned one after another, each on the terrain left by the roads before it, so roads in a shared corridor share a surface. The slope penalty rose from 25 to 300 (roads avoid ridges). Roads that end at a mountain city may cut and fill up to 45 m (was 20) and the carved bed may differ from the surface by up to 8 m (was 1) where roads cross on a steep slope. `road.planner` is v2.

## Consequences
- Easier: any hillside can hold a city; a new service is a YAML file; services can later be tied to NPCs, shops and tasks by ID.
- Harder: city meshes need the ground, so a city built without `settlements_ground.bin` falls back to level ground (and the game warns). The bake writes one more file (about 360 KB).
- Not done: a pier over water (no city stands next to water; the pier is for steep drops), interior floors on slopes (the floor is level), streets and paths (none are drawn now), collision for the landmark plinth ring on the far meshes.
- CLAUDE.md section 6.1 step 6 changes from "terrain flattened locally" to "built on the natural ground".
