# 0030. A first texture pass on the procedural building mesher
- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-11-01

## Context
Every building surface was flat vertex colour (known issues 39, 47, 60; `SettlementPalette`'s own doc comment calls its colours "placeholders until the retro art pass"). The developer asked for real, free textures on the buildings currently in the game, and to look nicer generally (walls, roofs, timber, stone).

[ADR 0015](0015-modular-building-kit.md) already plans an answer, but a bigger one: replace `BuildingMesher`'s procedural geometry with a hand-authored, tagged kit of pieces that the developer draws, so walls and roofs can tile along an arbitrary length. It is still `proposed`, needs art nobody has drawn yet, and is a different, much larger task than "texture the houses the generator already builds." Doing the ADR 0015 kit system was not what was asked for and was explicitly raised with and declined by the developer in favour of the smaller option below.

## Options considered
1. **Build the ADR 0015 kit system now** — the real long-term answer, but needs hand-drawn art and replaces the mesher; out of scope for a texture pass on the existing generator.
2. **A texture atlas** — one draw call, simple UVs, but ADR 0015 already ruled this out for the same reason it would apply here: walls and roof slopes vary in length per building, and an atlas sub-rect cannot repeat without bleeding into its neighbours.
3. **Texture the current procedural mesh with a `Texture2DArray`** (chosen) — keeps `BuildingMesher` exactly as it is except for naming which material each triangle is; each material is its own seamless, tiling PNG (no atlas problem); one `ShaderMaterial` with a texture array keeps the whole building (near or far) at one draw call.

## Decision
- **`Realm.Gen.Props.PropMaterial`**: a small enum (`None`, `WallPlaster`, `Mud`, `RoofThatch`, `RoofTileRed`, `RoofTileGrey`, `Wood`, `Stone`) naming what a triangle physically is. Gen stays Godot-free and opinion-free about rendering: it says *what the triangle is*, never *which pixels*. `PropTriangle` gained an optional `Material` field (default `None`, so every other mesher — bridges, walls, landmarks, roads, crowd — compiles and renders unchanged). `PropMeshBuilder.Triangle`/`Facing`/`FacingQuad`/`FacingPolygon`/`OrientedBox`/`YawBox`/`Prism` gained a matching optional parameter; only `BuildingMesher` and `FoundationMesher` pass one, at the specific calls that build an exterior wall, a roof (top and the new double-sided soffit), half-timbering, a door slab, or a stone plinth course.
- **Textures**: CC0, Kenney "Retro Textures Fantasy" (`src/Realm.Game/assets/buildings/kenney_retro-textures-fantasy/`, license and attribution in its `License.txt`), 64 px wide (128 px tall for the two that are naturally taller), nearest-filtered, chosen by eye from the pack against the roles above: `wall_brick_sand_center.png` (plaster), `floor_ground_dirt.png` (mud), `roof_thatch_center.png`, `roof_clay_red_center.png`, `roof_clay_grey_center.png` (cathedral), `floor_wood_planks.png` (beams, doors, the roof soffit), `wall_stone.png` (foundation plinth). This keeps ADR 0015's own file convention (seamless PNG, 64 or 128 px, nearest filtering, no mip baking) so nothing here is wasted if the kit system is built later — only the delivery mechanism (array layer vs. per-piece file) differs.
- **`PropMeshConverter.ToTexturedArrayMesh`** (`Realm.Game.Bridge`, new, alongside the existing untouched `ToArrayMesh`): computes UV by a per-triangle dominant-normal-axis planar projection of its own vertex positions (no authored UVs needed, and a building's own local origin gives each one a different texture phase for free) scaled by the material's tile size in metres (`BuildingMaterials.TileSize`), and writes the array layer (or -1 for `None`) into `UV2.x`. Only the two building-mesh conversions (`CityBuild`'s far mesh, `CityInstance`'s near mesh) call it; everything else keeps calling `ToArrayMesh` and the old `CityMaterials.Solid`, untouched.
- **`BuildingMaterials`** (`Realm.Game.World.Settlements`, new): loads the seven PNGs into one `Texture2DArray` (padding the 64x64 ones to 64x128 by tiling them, since every layer of a texture array must share one size) and builds one `ShaderMaterial`. The shader: `ALBEDO = layer < 0 ? vertex_colour : texture(array, vec3(UV, layer)).rgb * vertex_colour`, converting the vertex colour from sRGB to linear itself (the old `StandardMaterial3D` did this via `VertexColorIsSrgb`), `filter_nearest` on the sampler (the retro look, CLAUDE.md section 6.7), `ROUGHNESS = 1`, `METALLIC = 0` to match the material it replaces.

## Scope deliberately left out
Window and door frames, handles, interior walls and floors, city walls, bridges, landmarks, roads and the crowd stay flat vertex colour. Texturing those is more call sites for less visual return per this pass; nothing here blocks adding them later (`PropMaterial` and the `Texture2DArray` both have room, and the same converter method would just need wiring to more mesh builds).

## Consequences
- Easier: buildings look like something instead of flat coloured shapes, using assets that cost nothing and need no attribution; the texture roles map directly onto what a kit piece's material will eventually need (ADR 0015 is not contradicted, and its "texture supply" section gains a worked example of the format).
- Harder: a second, parallel rendering path for building meshes (`ToTexturedArrayMesh`/`BuildingMaterials.Textured`) exists alongside the original (`ToArrayMesh`/`CityMaterials.Solid`); a future contributor adding a new textured role has two places to touch (the `PropMaterial` enum and call site, and `BuildingMaterials.Layers`) instead of one.
- Not decided: whether ADR 0015's kit system is still the plan, or whether procedural generation plus a growing material list is now the long-term direction. Left for the developer; nothing here forces the choice.
