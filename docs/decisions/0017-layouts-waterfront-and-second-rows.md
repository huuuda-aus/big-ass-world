# 0017. Settlement layouts, waterfront settlements and a second row of houses
- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-22

## Context
Settlements were sparse (houses covered about a fifth of the walled ground), always radial, and kept clear of rivers and lakes. The developer asked for (1) a second row of houses, (2) cities on or beside rivers and lakes, with the wall cut off at the water, perhaps slightly in it, and (3) more than one layout: the existing radial one and a new cross layout like a crossroads, each quarter following the road to a different length according to the total number of buildings.

## Decision
- **Second row** (`CityGenerator.BuildLots`): a block is cut into rings. Row 1 faces the street (lots 10 m deep in rich wards, 6.5 m in slum wards); a 2.5 m lane runs behind it; row 2 faces the lane from the middle of the block. `RowCount` is 2 (a third row does not fit). A city of 250 houses now has about 14 wards and a wall radius of about 150 m (it was about 20 wards and 175 m).
- **Water** (`IWaterSampler`, `CityParams.Water`): the plan, wall outline and gates never depend on water, so the size and place of a settlement are the same with or without it. With water known, houses that touch it (a corner, a side midpoint or the centre) are dropped and farm buildings are not built in it. The wall mesher (`CityMesher.LandRuns`) tests the wall every 2 m and leaves the stretches in water out; a cut ends 2.5 m into the water with a tower (stone) or logs (palisade). The baked ground grid carries a water flag per 8 m point (river, lake or sea) from the same raster the road planner uses (`settlements_ground.bin` format 2); `SettlementGround` is both the ground and the water sampler.
- **Placement**: the centre (`WaterFreeCentreShare` 0.3 of the pad radius plus the clearance) must be dry; water may reach the outer wards. A site whose wall reaches water scores `WaterfrontBonus` (1.2) so waterfront sites win when they are about as gentle as the alternatives. Water is left out of the relief survey (a river bed lies far below its banks). `SETL005` is now only for water in the centre. `settlement.placement` is v6.
- **Layouts** (`SettlementLayout`, `Settlement.Layout`, baked in `settlements.json`): `Radial` is the existing generator. `Cross` (`CrossLayoutGenerator`): a 56 m square at the centre and four arms along the axes; each side of an arm is a strip with two rows of rectangular houses (the same lot rules as the radial layout); ward by distance along the arms (merchant, craftsmen, slum); the arm lengths come from random shares of a total chosen so the houses fit under the cap (`MaxBuildings`), so more houses mean longer arms and the arms differ in length; the wall is the plus-shaped outline round the arms with a gate in the middle of each arm's end, so roads arrive along the arm axes. Strips act as wards, so services, meshes, roads and the game need no special case. `SettlementSettings.CrossShare` (0.5, `--cross-share F`) is the share of cities and towns that get it; hamlets are always radial (too few houses for a cross).

**Revised 2026-10-23 (developer feedback: the cross-shaped cities were not liked):** the cross layout is now only for small settlements, those of at most 24 houses, which have no palisade (ADR 0016 now ties the palisade to a town of more than 24 houses); cities and towns are always radial. Hamlets (8 to 22 houses, chosen per hamlet) are crosses. Along each arm the chance of a house falls with the distance from the crossroads (row 1 from certain to 0.3, row 2 fading out by 55 percent of the arm), and a hamlet cross has shallower rows, a smaller square and shorter arms. `CrossShare` (default 1) is now the share of small settlements that are crosses. `settlement.placement` v9.

## Consequences
- Easier: another layout is another generator that fills the same `City`; water is one interface.
- Harder: the ground file changed format (old files are rejected; re-bake); a cross city with 250 houses is long (arms of 150 to 280 m, about 450 m across).
- Not done: a river through the middle of a city (the centre stays dry), bridges inside a city, side streets in the cross layout, a market square sized to the town, houses along the water's edge facing it.
