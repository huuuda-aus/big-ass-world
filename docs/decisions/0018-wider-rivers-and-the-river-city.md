# 0018. Wider rivers with a wobble, and a city halved by a river
- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-23

## Context
Rivers were painted 4 m wide and more, a ruled strip with no variation, and every settlement kept clear of them. The developer asked for rivers 3 to 4 times wider and variable (a very subtle sine wave as the base), and for one city to be halved by a river that runs through it untouched, with the houses spread over both banks, a minimum distance between the houses and the bank, and one or two arched bridges.

## Decision
- **Width** (`RiverWidth`): the base half width is max(7 m, 2.1 m x the square root of the flow in km2), 3.5 times the old formula. It is changed along the river by: the slope (gentle reaches are wider, steep ones narrower, a factor of 0.7 to 1.7), two layers of smooth noise (380 m and 130 m, plus or minus 28 and 10 percent, a different pattern for every river), pools (where the river is gentle it now and then opens into a wide still stretch, up to 1.9 times), a source that starts at 0.6 of the base, and at the end an estuary where a river reaches the sea (2.6 times wider over the last 420 m or 30 percent of its length) or a pond where it dries up (2.1 times). The result stays between 0.55 and 3.4 times the base and never changes by more than 12 percent between neighbouring points (32 m), so the shore is smooth. `RiverExtractor` stores the width in every `RiverPoint` (`HalfWidthMeters`; an unset point falls back to the base width). The baker's painted water, `RiverRaster`, `WaterMask`, the overview map (`HeightPreview`) and the width interpolation along each segment all read it, so there is one definition of how wide a river is.
- **Rivers reach water** (`ErosionSettings.RiversReachWater`, default true): once a vertex is part of a river, every vertex downstream of it is too, down to the sea, the world edge or the river it joins, however little flow is left. A river that loses water in a dry biome (`river_loss`) gets thin instead of drying up halfway, so rivers end only at the ocean, at the world's edge or at another river (lakes lie on the path: the flat water raises the slope factor, so a river widens as it comes into one). With the flag off the old `Dried` ending (with its pond) remains.
- **River city** (`SettlementSettings.RiverCities`, default 1; id `settlement.river_N`; `Settlement.IsRiverCity`): placed after the plain cities where the river passes within 70 m of the centre while the square and a 16 m ring round it are dry (tested on the 8 m `RiverRaster`); the nearer the river passes the centre and the longer its course through the pad, the better the site. The river is not touched: nothing is flattened, no house stands in it, the wall is cut at the water (a water gate, ADR 0017) and the plan, wall outline and gates are the same with or without water. The plan is `RiverCityPlanFactor` (1.35) times larger than the house cap, because the river and the bank buffer take houses away; the houses left on the two banks come to about the cap.
- **Bank buffer** (`CityParams.WaterBufferMeters`, 8 m by default, applied to every settlement that has water): a house, or a farm building, is dropped when any corner, side midpoint or its centre is in water or within the buffer of it (eight directions are tested round each point).
- **Bridges** (`CityBridgeFinder`, `City.Bridges`, `CityParams.MaxBridges` 2): where a street crosses water for 6 to 110 m, a bridge runs from 3 m before the water to 3 m after it; the two crossings nearest the centre that are at least 35 m apart are kept. `ArchBridgeMesher` builds a 7 m roadway that rises to a crown of a tenth of the span (1.2 to 6 m) along a parabola, with an arched underside (thinner at the crown than at the ends), spandrel walls, 1 m stone parapets and an abutment at each end. The roadway meets the ground at both ends (0.05 m above it) and its slope never exceeds about 22 degrees, so the walker crosses without jumping; the same mesh is the collision. `SETL010` reports a river city with no river near its centre.
- **Bridge abutments**: the timber road bridge's stone abutments used to be flush with the road, so their tops showed through and clipped the graded terrain at the ends. They now sit under the ends of the deck, stop 0.45 m below the roadway and are 0.2 m narrower than it; the arched city bridge's abutments are under the roadway too (inside the bridge, not behind its ends).
- `settlement.placement` is v8.

## Consequences
- Easier: width is one function; any layout can have bridges and a bank buffer.
- Harder: wider rivers make longer road bridges; the shipped world still passes `ROAD` validation with the 80 m limit. The plan factor is a tuning number, not a guarantee of the house count.
- Not done: the river itself is still painted colour (no water surface, no swimming or current), quays and houses facing the water, a market on the bank, more than two bridges, bridges for the roads outside the cities as arches, a river through the centre square.
