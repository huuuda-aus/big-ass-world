# 2026-11-01 — watchtowers behind the palisade

## Goal
The developer pointed out that a town's wooden watchtowers stood outside the palisade — defensively backwards; everything should be behind the gates except the specific buildings (farmer, forester, etc.) that are deliberately outside already. In a follow-up, they asked for two more things: the front two posts should be part of the wall rather than standing apart from it, and a hole and ladder in the floor the player can use (fade transition, no climbing animation) to reach the platform.

## What was done

### Round 1: pulling the tower behind the wall
- Found the cause: `CityMesher.Watchtower` placed each tower's position *on* the wall line (zero perpendicular offset, same as the fence logs themselves), tangentially offset from the gate along the wall by a fixed `GateWidthMeters / 2 + WatchtowerSide / 2`. The tower's 3.4 m square footprint straddled the line, half in, half out.
- First pass: pull the tower inward, perpendicular to the specific edge it stands beside (not straight at the city's centre — a wall segment is not generally radial, and a centroid-direction pull reduces the along-wall clearance already kept from the gate, which broke an existing test, `Palisade_IsWoodAndLowerThanAStoneWall_WithGatesLeftOpen`).
- That surfaced a second bug on a wider seed sweep: the watchtower platform is axis-aligned (`YawBox(..., yaw: 0, ...)`), so its farthest corner from its centre, along an arbitrary direction, is its half-*diagonal* (2.4 m), not its half-width (1.7 m) — a fixed inset sized for the half-width still let a corner poke back out on some wall angles. Fixed by building the tower facing the wall edge it stands beside (`Watchtower` now takes a `forward` direction) instead of always facing world axes, so its reach past the wall line is bounded by its half-width regardless of the wall's own angle.
- A wider sweep still (up to 300 seeds) found a third, deeper issue: the fixed tangential offset assumes the wall edge beside the gate is at least `GateWidthMeters / 2 + WatchtowerSide / 2` (5.7 m) long. A short connecting segment near a tight ward corner (one case was 0.63 m) is not — the "on wall" anchor point then lands past the real corner, off the edge the perpendicular math reasons about, onto a differently-angled segment beyond it. Clamped the along-wall offset to the real edge length; where even that leaves no room for the tower (the floor for minimum clearance from the gate exceeds the edge length), that side is skipped rather than forced into a bad position — the gate still has its other watchtower.
- As a last-resort safety net beyond the analytic margin, `PlaceWatchtower` now verifies the actual footprint (all four platform corners, not just the centre) against the real wall polygon with a general point-in-polygon test (`PolygonMath.Contains`, new: even-odd ray casting, correct for the wall's non-convex shape, unlike `ConvexPoly`) and nudges further inward in 0.3 m steps, up to 20, if it still doesn't fit. Belt and braces: the analytic clamp handles the ordinary cases cheaply; the nudge loop catches whatever local wall shape the analytic reasoning didn't anticipate.

### Round 2: tied into the wall, a floor hole and a climbable ladder
- `PlaceWatchtower`'s inset shrank to just the platform's half-width minus a small overlap (1.5 m, was 2.6 m): the platform's front edge now sits flush with, and a little past, the wall line instead of leaving a gap behind it. The front two posts are no longer built at all — the palisade's own logs stand where they would. Only the back two posts, and two new diagonal braces from the wall up to the platform's front corners, need to clear the wall; `PlaceWatchtower`'s nudge loop (`BackCornersInside`, renamed from `FootprintInside`) now checks only those two back corners, since the front ones are meant to meet the wall, not clear it.
- The floor is no longer a single slab: `FloorWithHole` builds it as four non-overlapping strips round a 1 m square hole in the middle. `LadderProp` puts two rails and rungs straight up through it, from the ground to just under the platform — a prop only, with no physical climbing collision, matching the developer's "no climbing animation necessary."
- `Realm.Gen.Settlements.Model.Ladder` (plan position, bottom height, top height) is collected per watchtower in `Palisade`, returned alongside the mesh from a new internal `WallAndLadders` (the public `Wall()` is now a thin wrapper over it, so its many existing callers, tests included, are unaffected), and exposed on `CityMeshes.Ladders` (defaults to empty, so `Build`'s only other caller needs no change).
- Threaded `Ladders` through to the game: `CityBuild.Ladders` (local frame, populated from `CityMesher.Build`'s result) and `CityInstance.Ladders` (world space, converted with `Settlement.ToWorld` plus `GroundHeightMeters`, matching the convention used elsewhere in `CityInstance` rather than relying on the node being attached to the tree).
- Built the interaction itself in `WorldScene`, since the codebase had no interact-prompt, fade or teleport system yet (checked first, see Problems below) to reuse: `UpdateLadderPrompt` (called every frame alongside the existing `UpdatePersonLabel`) checks the player's distance to every loaded city's ladder ends (plain per-frame math, the same style as `CityInstance.Pick`/`Challenged`, not an `Area3D`) and shows a prompt label within 2 m; `UseLadder`, bound to a new one-shot `E` key case in `_Input` (guarded `!_fly`, since `E` is already the continuous fly-mode "rise" key read in `Move`), runs a `Tween` that fades a full-screen `ColorRect` to black, sets `_body.Position` to the far end plus a small lift (the same pattern the existing fall-through recovery in `Move` already uses: set position, zero velocity, let `MoveAndSlide`'s floor snap settle it), then fades back in.
- Gave the back posts a distinct colour (`Log(32)`, not `Log(0)`) so a regression test could find them unambiguously (see Problems below); the braces and ladder keep the low hash values already used elsewhere in a palisade, since no test needs to isolate them.

### Round 3: the developer actually climbed one
Confirmed by play, not by the review this devlog said was still outstanding: climbing up left the player stuck. Two real bugs, both in the platform's own geometry, not the interaction code:
- The top landing point was the ladder's own plan position — the exact centre of the floor's hole, where there is no floor. `Ladder` gained a separate `TopPosition`, offset onto the back strip (`LadderTopLandingInsetMeters`, 1.05 m: half the hole plus the player's body radius plus a margin) instead of sitting in the gap.
- Headroom under the roof was 1.55 m (roof base `WatchtowerHeight`, 6.5 m, minus the standing surface, 4.95 m) against a 1.8 m player capsule — the player's head was 0.25 m into the cone roof's collision the moment they arrived. Raised `WatchtowerHeight` to 7.2 m (2.25 m of clearance). `Realm.Gen` has no reference to the actual player capsule (it stays Godot-free), so both fixed numbers are only as good as a comment saying why; a new test pins the 1.8 m figure down so the two cannot drift apart silently again.

### Round 4: a railing
The developer asked for a waist-high wall round the platform, having nothing to stop a fall off the edge (which is now reachable). `Railing`/`RailRun` (`CityMesher`), posts and a top bar round all four edges, the same style and height (1 m) as `FoundationMesher.Deck`'s existing pier rail, reused rather than invented fresh. Sits on the floor's own top surface, clear of the braces (whose top end is just under the floor) and well clear of the roof.

### Round 5: filling the rail
A single thin top bar left the rail mostly open underneath. Replaced it with horizontal log courses (round `Tube`s) between the posts on every edge — round and laid on their side, like the palisade's own fence logs but at half the diameter (`WatchtowerRailLogRadius`, derived from `LogMinRadius`/`LogMaxRadius`), not the flat plank the first pass used. First pass: three fixed courses at 20/50/80% of the rail's height, which still left visible gaps between them.

### Round 6: no gaps
The developer asked for the courses stacked tight enough to show no gap at all. `RailCourseHeights` now computes however many courses it takes to stack from the floor to the top with each one spaced at 92% of a log's diameter (a slight overlap, not just touching at one point, so it reads as solid rather than a row of circles meeting edge-to-edge) — about 5 courses at the current radius and rail height, up from the fixed 3.

## Decisions made (link ADRs)
No new ADR for round 1 (a geometry bug fix with no design choice attached beyond the developer's own stated principle). Round 2 is a larger addition (a new interaction system, Gen-to-Game data channel) but still implementation detail under the developer's own explicit spec (front posts merge with the wall; hole and ladder; fade transition; no climbing animation; use at both ends) rather than a new design decision of its own — no ADR either, though a future ADR on interaction/UI conventions in general (prompts, fades, teleports) would have this as its first worked example if one is ever written.

## Problems encountered and how they were solved
- Each of the three round-1 bugs was only found by progressively stronger testing: a single fixture (`Town`, seed 8) passed after the first fix; a 7-seed sweep caught the second; a 40-to-300-seed sweep caught the third. A single-seed regression test would have shipped a tower that still pokes outside the wall on an unlucky city layout.
- The first attempt at a regression test asserted every outset corner is *at least* some distance from every original wall edge — backwards (same mistake, independently, as the roof-overhang test earlier today): the true constraint is *at most* that distance from the edges not adjacent to the point in question, since a point on one edge is unrelated to a far edge's offset line. Replaced with a direct point-in-polygon check against the real wall shape, which needs no such reasoning about which edges are "nearby."
- In round 2, making the front posts optional changed what "correctly placed" means (the front corners are now meant to sit at or past the wall line, not inside it), which broke the round-1 regression test by design, not by regression. Rather than loosen the check, narrowed it to what is still unconditionally true: the two back posts. That in turn needed the posts to be unambiguously identifiable in the mesh — which surfaced a real, if harmless, pre-existing fact about `SettlementPalette.Log`: its shade index is `(hash >> 5) % shades.Length`, so every hash under 32 (0, 1, 2, as used by the fence, the gate beam, and the new braces and ladder) collapses to the same shade. Posts now use `Log(32)` so they are both findable by colour and a shade apart from everything else, by design rather than accident.
- Researched the codebase (a dedicated subagent, read-only) before writing any interaction code: confirmed there is no existing "press E to interact" system, `Area3D` trigger, fade/respawn mechanic, or `Tween` usage anywhere in `Realm.Game`, and no custom Input Map (all input is raw key polling in `WorldScene`). Built on the closest existing idioms instead of inventing new ones: `CityInstance.Pick`/`Challenged`'s plain-math proximity style, and `Move`'s fall-recovery snippet for safely repositioning the player.
- Round 2 shipped with no visual check of its own (no desktop screenshot tool this session, noted at the time) and both round-3 bugs would have been caught by one: a human standing on the platform notices a floor that isn't there and a roof that clips their head immediately. The geometric tests added afterwards (hole clearance, roof headroom) encode exactly what looking at it would have shown; they exist because the developer looked at it, not instead of that.

## Tests added
`FortificationTests.Watchtowers_BackPostsStandInsideThePalisade` (30-seed sweep, renamed from the round-1 `..._NotStraddlingOrOutsideIt`, now checking only the two back posts via their `Log(32)`, height-filtered top-cap triangles). `Build_WithAPalisade_ExposesLadders_OneAtEveryWatchtower_ReachingFromGroundToThePlatform` (now also checks `TopPosition` clears the hole and lies inside the wall) and `Build_WithAStoneWall_HasNoLadders`. `Build_WithAPalisade_TheRoofClearsAStandingPlayerAboveTheLadder` (round 3). `Build_WithAPalisade_HasAWaistHighRailingAroundThePlatform` (round 4; round 5 added a triangle-count floor to the same test, since posts and the fill courses share a colour and a height range and are otherwise hard to tell apart from mesh data alone; round 6 raised that floor again, 300 to 500, now that there are about 5 courses instead of 3 — a weaker proxy for "no gap" than measuring the actual spacing, but `RailCourseHeights` is private and the triangle count at least moves the right way when the stacking gets denser). `Palisade_IsWoodAndLowerThanAStoneWall_WithGatesLeftOpen` unchanged and still passing. Full `Realm.Gen.Tests`: 649 passed. The game-side interaction (`WorldScene`) has no automated test (no Godot smoke-test harness covers input/UI yet); verified by a clean game log (no shader/scene errors) after each rebuild, and visual review was handed to the developer, since this session has no desktop screenshot tool.

## Open questions / next steps
- The same `PolygonMath.Contains` is now available for anything else in `Realm.Gen.Settlements` that needs a correct inside/outside test against the (possibly non-convex) wall or a similar polygon, instead of assuming convexity.
- Stone city walls were not touched: their towers straddle the wall line by design (real bastions do), which the developer's request was not about. They also get no ladder: the only way up is a wall-walk, not modelled yet.
- `WorldScene`'s new prompt/fade/teleport code is the first of its kind in the project; if more interactions are added later (doors, levers, NPC dialogue triggers), it is worth lifting the pattern into a small reusable helper instead of copying it, and deciding then whether `Area3D` trigger volumes are worth the switch from plain per-frame distance checks.
- No automated coverage of the actual player-facing behaviour (prompt shows/hides correctly, E teleports to the right end, the fade times out) — there is no Godot-side smoke-test harness for input/UI in this project yet to write one against.
